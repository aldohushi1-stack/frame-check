## Frame check: Henley Bike Repairs

**The claim:** Fixed prices are clear, and jobs are quick.

**Frames spotted:** Almost none. This is a plain price list.

| Words | Frame | Does the flip change a decision? |
|---|---|---|
| "Parts extra, quoted before we start" | Omission (mild) | Only for the Full service, and the text discloses it up front. |

Also noticed:
- "Puncture repair: $25 (tube included)" is a real inclusion, not a trick.
- "Most jobs done the same day; we'll ring you if it's going to be longer" is a soft promise. It isn't a guarantee.
- There's no urgency, no anchor price, no "from" or "up to", and no social proof.

**Flip it:** "Full service: $180 plus whatever parts it needs." The $180 is a floor, not a total. A worn chain, cables or tyres could add a lot.

**Neutral numbers:**
- Puncture: $25 all-in.
- Standard: $95 flat. The text doesn't say whether it covers parts, so assume it doesn't.
- Full service: $180 plus parts. The extra $85 over Standard buys wheel truing and a bearing regrease. The final bill is unknown until they quote.
- The text gives no parts prices, so I can't work out a typical Full service total.

**Questions to ask them:**
1. What do parts usually add to a Full service? Can you give me a typical range?
2. Does the Standard service include small parts such as cables and brake pads, or is it labour only?
3. If the quote comes back too high, is there a fee to decline the work?
4. What does "longer" mean for jobs that aren't done the same day? Is it a day or a week?
5. Is the $25 puncture price per tyre?

**Verdict: Fair.** The facts hold and the flip doesn't change a sensible decision. The one catch is that "parts extra" means the Full service price is open-ended. The quote-before-work promise limits that risk.

**Next step:** Ask question 1 before you book a Full service. For a puncture or Standard service, just go.
