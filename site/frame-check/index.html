What the skill says when you paste an ad and ask "is this a good deal?" — taken from a real run, unedited except for length. The ad is a composite written for the test, not a real retailer.

The ad

MEGA CLEARANCE — 4 DAYS ONLY!
3-seater leather lounge. Was $3,999. NOW $1,499!
Save $2,500 — that's over 60% off.
Or just $15 a week with 0% interest for 24 months*.
Australia's #1 leather lounge — loved by over 50,000 families.
Only 6 left in store. Sale ends Sunday.

*Approved applicants. $49 account fee and $9.95 monthly fee apply. 0% for 24 months, then 29.99% p.a.

Frame Check

Verdict: Pushy but true
  • Omission. "0% interest isn't 0% cost." Paid as advertised, finance comes to about $1,848 against $1,499 cash — roughly $290–$350 in fees.
  • Anchor. "The maths is right (2,500 ÷ 3,999 = 62.5%), but the 'was' price is the claim to test."
  • The question to ask: "How many were sold at $3,999 in the last 6 months?"
  • The line: "It would tip into misleading if the lounge was never really sold at $3,999."

Ten frames. One question each.

Words edit what people remember and what they choose — Loftus & Palmer showed it in 1974 with one verb, "smashed" against "hit". These are the ten frames Frame Check names, and the flip that takes the shine off.

FrameLooks likeThe question
Gain vs loss"you're losing customers every day"Told the other way, is it still true for you?
Relative vs absolute"cuts your risk by 20%"From what starting number?
Small unit"$15 a week", "less than a coffee a day"What's the total over the full term?
"Up to" / "from""save up to $400", "from $99"What does a typical person get?
Anchor"was $3,999, now $1,499"Did anyone ever pay the first price?
Urgency / scarcity"only 3 left", "ends Sunday"What actually happens if you wait?
Social proof"loved by 50,000 families"How many, named, and checkable?
Loaded words"rarely offered", "investment"What's the plainest word for it?
Omission / defaultthe asterisk, the fine printWhat's missing from the headline?
Authority"studies show", "experts say"Which study, how many people, who paid?

When you're the one writing, every frame also has to pass four questions: True? Survives daylight? Serves them too? Leaves the past alone? Fail one and it's marked REJECTED, with the reason.

Questions

Is this a lie detector?

No. A frame is a choice of words, not proof of intent. Plenty of honest sellers say "from $99". The verdict is about one thing: whether seeing the flip would change your decision.

Is it legal advice?

No. It won't tell you whether a claim breaks consumer law. It tells you which question to ask before you sign.

Does my text go anywhere?

The checker runs entirely in your browser — it makes no network requests once the page has loaded, and the repo has a test that fails if it ever does. The skill runs inside your own agent, so your text goes wherever your agent already sends it, and nowhere else.

Why are there two parts?

The checker is for the moment you're holding an offer and want a quick look. The skill is for the full read — the maths, the questions to ask, the verdict — and for writing your own words honestly.

Can I make it follow my own rules?

Yes. Copy house-rules.example.md to house-rules.md beside the skill and add your standing rules: your voice, your channels, the kind of ask you prefer.

What does it cost?
