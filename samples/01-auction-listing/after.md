## The claim
Act now. This is a rare, in-demand home, and "$890k" is the price you should expect to pay.

## Frames spotted

| Words in the ad | Frame | Flip |
|---|---|---|
| "offers above $890,000" | **Anchor** | It's a floor, not an estimate. The ad itself says nearby homes sold "up to $1.2m", so the realistic range may be well above $890k. |
| "sold for up to $1.2m" | **"Up to"** | That's the best comparable, not the typical one. It gives no addresses, dates, block sizes or condition. |
| "potential rental return of up to $850 per week" | **"Up to" + small unit** | It's a best case, quoted weekly. |
| "Extensive interest", "EOI close Wednesday", "will sell before auction day" | **Urgency / social proof** | None of it is quantified. Nothing says how many parties or how many inspections. "Unless sold prior" pushes you to bid early, before you've had time to check anything. |
| "Rarely offered", "most tightly held pockets" | **Loaded words** | Estate-agent boilerplate. It tells you nothing checkable. |
| "Some original features retained" | **Omission** | This could mean dated bathrooms, wiring or plumbing. Only the kitchen is called renovated. |

## Flip it
"A 650m² house expected to sell somewhere between about $890k and $1.2m or more, with an unquantified claim of strong interest, pressuring you to commit before the auction."

## Neutral numbers
- **Rent:** $850 × 52 = $44,200 a year, assuming it's rented all year.
  - On $890k, that's 4.97% gross.
  - On $1.2m, it's 3.7% gross.
  - Council rates, insurance, management fees (often 5–8% of rent), maintenance and vacancy all come off that. Net yield is likely a point or more lower. I can't work it out because the ad gives none of those costs.
- **Price gap:** $1.2m − $890k = $310k, or 35% above the guide. The ad leaves open where in that range it will land.
- **Date problem:** 15 November 2026 is a Sunday, but the ad says Saturday. 15 November was a Saturday in 2025. Check that the ad is current and the date is right. A stale listing would also undercut the urgency.

## Questions to ask the agent
1. What is the agent's own estimated selling range, and what's the reserve? (Reserve can't be disclosed, but you can ask where they expect it to land.)
2. Which three comparable sales are they using? Ask for addresses, dates, land size and condition.
3. How many registered bidders or EOIs are there, and how many inspections?
4. Is the $850 a current appraisal, or an actual lease? What is the property management fee?
5. What condition are the original parts in (wiring, plumbing, roof)? Is there a building and pest report or a title and zoning check?

## Verdict
**Pushy but true.** Nothing here is false, but the guide price is a floor that sits well below the agent's own comparables. The "up to" figures and the unquantified demand claims all push the same way. Don't treat $890k as your budget. Budget toward $1.2m or more, or walk away.

**Next step:** send questions 1 and 2 to the agent before the EOI deadline, and don't commit until you have real comparables.
