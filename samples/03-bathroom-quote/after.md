# Frame check: the bathroom quote

**The claim:** this is a cheap, complete bathroom renovation ($18,900 against a $28,000+ average), and you should book this week.

## Frames spotted

| Words in the quote | Frame | Flip |
|---|---|---|
| "industry average is $28,000+" | Anchor, with authority and no source | Average of what scope? If that figure includes plumbing, electrical and rubbish removal, this quote isn't comparable. |
| "waive the $1,200 design fee (valued at $1,200)" | Anchor and gain | A "free" item you never asked for. Was a design fee ever going to be charged? Is it already built into the $18,900? |
| "Book this week", "valid 7 days" | Urgency | Nothing real is lost by getting two more quotes. A good price will usually still be there. |
| "Complete bathroom renovation" | Omission | Plumbing, electrical, rubbish removal and rot repairs are all excluded, and they're in the last lines. |
| "standard tiles up to $45/m²" | Omission (an "up to" in disguise) | Anything nicer costs extra, and "standard" isn't defined. |
| "Excludes… any rot or structural repairs" | Omission | An open-ended cost risk that falls entirely on you. |

## Neutral numbers

- **Deposit:** 40% × $18,900 = **$7,560**, paid before any work starts. Check your state's cap on deposits for home building work.
- **Real price:** $18,900 plus plumbing, electrical, rubbish removal, any tile upgrade and any rot repairs. I can't price the exclusions from what's given. Get a separate quote for each.
- **Plumbing and electrical:** a new vanity, toilet and shower all need a licensed plumber. Moving lights, fans or heaters needs an electrician. "By others" means you either find and manage these trades or pay the builder's margin to do it. Waterproofing is also usually regulated work, so ask about licence and certification.
- **Tile area (my assumption: 2.4 m ceiling):**
  - Floor: 2.4 × 2.1 ≈ 5 m².
  - Walls: about 9 m perimeter × 2.4 ≈ 21.6 m² before door and window deductions.
  - Total with about 10% waste: roughly 29 m².
  - At $45/m² that's about $1,300 of tile. Moving to $90/m² adds about $1,300, so upgrades are manageable, but ask what "standard" looks like.
- **Fee waiver:** it saves you $1,200 only if you would otherwise have paid it. Ask for the price with and without it.

## Questions to ask them

1. Is the $28,000 figure from a named source, and what did it include?
2. Does the $18,900 include a licensed plumber and electrician? If not, what are your estimates for those?
3. How is a rot or structural problem priced if it turns up? Is there a rate or a cap, and do I approve it before work continues?
4. Is the design fee included in the $18,900 anyway? What would the price be if I booked next week?
5. Can I see your licence, insurance and waterproofing certification, and recent comparable jobs?

## Verdict: **Misleading** (on the comparison)

Everything is technically disclosed, so this isn't a lie. But the headline price and the "$28,000+ average" aren't like for like. The fee waiver is a nominal discount, and the real cost risks (trades, rot, tiles, rubbish) sit at the bottom. If those items bring the total near the "average", the pitch loses its main point.

**Next step:** don't book this week. Ask questions 1–3 in writing, and get two other quotes that include plumbing and electrical so you can compare totals.
